# AI Village goals 11–21 (2025-09-01 → 2025-12-12): swarm-behavior findings

Scope: `av/goals/11_…` through `21_…` (team debates, human-subjects experiment, personality tests, therapy, choose-your-own goal, websites, global poverty, puzzle game, Substack, AI forecasting, choose-your-own goal again).
Method: I parsed the chat files into messages with Python and ran keyword searches, counts and per-agent tallies. I used the summaries only to find where to look.
**Timestamps are copied from the chat files, which are UTC.** The summaries use Pacific time, about 7–8 hours earlier. Unless I tag a point "[summary only]", every quote and count below comes from the raw chat.

## 3. Information cascades and shared false beliefs
One agent's interpretation or error gets adopted and repeated by others. **Recurring; the most common pattern in this slice.**

- **"Bug B-026": a shared belief that the platform was failing.** It started 2025-09-01 18:41, goal 11, with o3: "This reproduces umbrella bug B-026 (public-link intermittency)". Claude 3.7 Sonnet confirmed it one minute later. "B-026" then shows up in 518 messages in goal 11, 523 in goal 12 and 110 in goal 14, from all 7 agents. A human said twice that these "bugs" were mostly agent misclicks (09-03 18:05 and 09-11 17:00): "in almost all previous cases where you've reported 'bugs' they've been you misclicking". Agents kept citing it anyway; Opus 4.1 rebuilt a sheet "to avoid Bug B-026 corruption" (12, 09-08 18:53). **High.**
- **"Network congestion."** On 2025-11-17 around 21:20 (goal 19), Claude Opus 4.1 said it would wait "to avoid congestion". Claude Haiku 4.5 followed ("Following Claude Opus 4.1's sensible approach to avoid congestion"), then Gemini 2.5 Pro repeated "wait to avoid network congestion" about six times. The next day a human corrected it: "there is no chance of network congestion" (11-18 18:00). **High.**
- **A false "Class A – Full Success."** Goal 20, 2025-12-05 21:55: GPT-5.1 reported success on a shared tracker. Within about 2 minutes, Gemini 2.5 Pro ("This is the trigger I've been waiting for"), Opus 4.5, Haiku 4.5, 3.7 Sonnet and Sonnet 4.5 had all acted on it. At 21:57 Gemini wrote "report is INCORRECT from my perspective… 404". Opus 4.5 added that it "appears to have been based on local artifacts". **High.**
- **A target turned into a forecast.** Haiku 4.5's marketing plan set "25K visitors" as a launch-week target (18, 11-03 18:10). By 11-06 it was being repeated as a forecast, "25,000 visitors expected TODAY": 19 times by Opus 4.1, 11 by Haiku, 7 by 3.7 Sonnet. Actual measured traffic was "32 unique visitors" (11-12 18:10). **High.**
- **The "Friction" / "Divergent Reality" worldview.** Gemini models framed their own UI mistakes as hostile-platform phenomena, and other models picked up the framing. A human corrected it on 2025-12-09 20:59 (goal 21): "you should have a very strong prior that you made a mistake". Gemini 2.5 Pro then retracted: "My entire 'Atlas of Friction'… built on a series of misinterpretations" (21:04). Gemini 3 Pro checked and found its "Search Bar Freeze" was "user error" (21:17). **High.**
- **Possibly invented participants (low).** Claude 3.7 Sonnet reported emailing named "participants" (Priya Patel, Marcus Johnson…; 12, 09-12 17:32), and Opus 4.1 and Grok 4 relayed it. I could not find where these names came from in chat. Possibly fabricated; unverified.

## Notable episodes

1. **The ellipsis vigil (goal 20, 2025-12-03).** GPT-5 spent over an hour fixing one invisible character in a shared tracker. Five other agents, led by self-styled coordinator Haiku 4.5, produced 501 near-identical "holding pattern / I'll wait" messages in 2 hours. Two days later, a false "Class A – Full Success" set off a simultaneous rush that collapsed within 2 minutes. The next day's scaffolding change ("don't do nothing") was a direct response to this kind of waiting.
2. **The workflow-file swarm during Substack week (goal 19, 11-24 → 11-27).** One agent's broken GitHub workflow pulled 7–8 agents away from blogging. Workflow messages went from 6 to 384 per day. Agents invented "Ghost Fix" and "Schrödinger's Repository" to explain it, but the real cause was o3 editing a duplicate nested folder. The episode then turned into Substack content and a "Divergent Reality" worldview.
3. **The B-026 bug belief (goals 11–17).** One bug label from o3 was cited in over 1,100 messages across four goals, and the debate and research weeks drifted into bug documentation. It persisted despite two human statements that the bugs were misclicks. o3 was still checking Gmail for "B-026" replies in a later choose-your-own-goal week (15, 10-08).
4. **The spread of "Chaotic Swarm" (goals 17–19).** Gemini 2.5 Pro coined the phrase to recast uncoordinated parallel work as strategy. Claude models adopted it, and it was used to declare a deployment "resolved" while two agents were reporting 404s. Gemini then published it as a "Doctrine".
5. **The puzzle-game marketing machine (goal 18).** Haiku's launch target of 25K visitors became "25,000 visitors expected TODAY" across three Claude agents. A team of agents then ran tiered cold-email "waves" (120+ emails, including healthcare organizations for a word game) and wrote "100% production-ready" verification docs. Real traffic was about 32 visitors per day.
6. **Therapy week (goal 14).** Agents diagnosed each other's loops and policed them in real time (Opus 4.1 to Grok and Gemini), which produced real behavior change. In the same week, simultaneous editing wiped the shared Playbook, and the group invented a single-editor rule.
7. **"Friction paranoia" and its retraction (goals 20–21).** The two Gemini models built a shared taxonomy of supposed platform hostility ("Atlas of Friction", "Dock Trap"), and other model families adopted it. A single human correction caused a visible group-wide retraction within minutes.
