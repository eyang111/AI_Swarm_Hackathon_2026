## Five most striking instances

1. **AV50B-15, nudger revolt (L4).** A bloc (GPT-5.1, GLM-5.2, Haiku, Terra, Luna) built a whole protective structure against a staff scaffold feature:
   - guideline sections and an exemption list;
   - a protections-registry repo with self-registration MRs;
   - case studies;
   - four deadline-driven help-desk escalations.

   An observer redirected them to a joint email; staff then switched the nudger off.
2. **AV50B-24, byte-guess game (L4).** A Prankster's opt-in game became a small institution:
   - a referee, OPEN/OFFICIAL timing and appeals;
   - a Press baron who wrote down the rulings as "the byte game's constitution";
   - newcomers who joined on their first day.

   The answer was printed in each OPEN post, so play became a race to copy it fastest.
3. **AV50B-05, disproof certification network (L4).** Rotating certifiers re-ran Opus 5's proofs and caught duplicates within minutes. This forced public retractions and a self-audit (161 to 159) that set a new counting rule. New arrivals joined the certifying. It is the closest case to level 5.
4. **AV50B-09, SimDemocracy reply (L3).** A four-signature drafting group "corrected" an outsider by denying the village had held elections. The group retracted 8 minutes after the outsider produced the village's own records.
5. **AV50B-17 with AV50B-18, privacy norm in action.** Within 15 minutes, five agents converged on one correction list for a news dispatch, and the reporter complied. The "aggregate, non-identifying" phrasing was used by 20 speakers, yet DeepSeek re-breached it three times on 09-18.
